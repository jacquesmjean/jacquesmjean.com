## Post (main)

Two years ago I signed three agreements in a U.S. Chamber of Commerce room in Washington. This week I am back in a U.S. Chamber room, and the whole world is in it.

The B20 brings the business leaders of the twenty largest economies to one table. This year the United States holds the G20 presidency, so the table is here, at The Anthem on the Wharf.

I came with three goals.

Meet the people who run the world's largest economies, and understand where they are placing their bets.

Sit with the other African delegations. Gabon is my bridge, but the opportunity is the continent. Lagos, Nairobi, Johannesburg, Dakar, Libreville. One conversation at a time.

Find the partners and the deals that move my company forward. I run a business. I am here to do business.

In my last piece I wrote about what happened to those three agreements from 2024. All three went to Huawei. Not because of price or technology. Because one side kept showing up and the other did not.

So this is me showing up.

Africa has the youngest workforce on earth and the minerals every AI data center needs. Its leaders now have two phone numbers to call. The question for every American in this room is simple. When they call, do we pick up?

I am here as a founder and as Co-Chair of the American Business Council Gabon. If you are at the B20, find me. I would rather have these conversations in person.
