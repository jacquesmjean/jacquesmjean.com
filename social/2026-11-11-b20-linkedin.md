## Post (main)

Two years ago I signed three agreements in a U.S. Chamber of Commerce room in Washington. This week I am back in a U.S. Chamber room, and the whole world is in it.

The B20 brings the business leaders of the twenty largest economies to the same table. This year the United States holds the G20 presidency, so the table is here.

I came for one reason. To make sure Africa is at it.

In my last piece I wrote about what happened to those three agreements. All three went to Huawei. Not because of price or technology. Because one side kept showing up and the other did not.

A summit is showing up. So is an ambassador in the chair. So is a company sending four people to a round table instead of one.

Africa has the youngest workforce on earth, the minerals every AI data center needs, and leaders who now have two phone numbers to call. The question for every American in this room is simple. When they call, do we pick up?

I am here as a founder and as Co-Chair of the American Business Council Gabon. If you are at the B20 and you care about Africa, find me. I would rather have that conversation in person.
