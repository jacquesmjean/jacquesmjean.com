## Post

I have sat in that hotel lobby. São Paulo, Prague, a dozen cities.

The CEO flies in for a town hall or an acquisition visit. The day is scripted to the minute. Then at eleven at night the executive team is in the lobby with no protocol at all, and a stranger at the next table already knows the CEO's name.

I started my career as a cybersecurity analyst at Honeywell. In 25 years of running technology across 15 countries, I never once saw an executive team briefed on this before a trip. We protected the laptops better than the people carrying them.

So when Jensen Huang resigned yesterday, I was not shocked. I was angry.

He owns his choices. That is not the question. The question is how the one man who decided who gets AI chips was left exposed for years, when Washington was warned in October 2025 and did nothing.

We classify the chips. We did not classify the man who decides who gets them.

I wrote down what boards should do Monday morning, what Washington should have done a year ago, and why the real problem is not one man but one point of failure.

https://jacquesmjean.com/perspectives/most-valuable-man-in-america-had-no-security-detail/

Who is your Jensen? And who is protecting him?
